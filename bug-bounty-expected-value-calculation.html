Worked example of the formula shape

Take the formula symbolically. EV = p × payout − cost. Suppose p = 0.05 and payout = X and cost = C. Then EV = 0.05X − C. If X is 20 times C, EV is exactly zero, meaning the campaign breaks even in expectation. Every multiple of C above 20 pushes EV positive; below, negative. That breakeven multiple is the fastest sanity check before you spend a week on a program. Note what the formula does not include: dedupes, triage rejections, and disclosure lockouts all sit inside your real p, which is why honest p is low. Why most hunters earn nothing

Bug bounty pools are winner-take-most. With 234 active programs (Immunefi public bounty API) and many hunters competing per program, the first valid report on a bug takes the payout and everyone else on the same bug gets nothing. Your EV calculation has to price dedupes inside p, not after it.

Why program count matters

More programs mean more surface area where a bug may not yet be found, which is the only lever that raises p. Tracking the count via Immunefi's public API (Immunefi) tells you how the pool is growing; a shrinking pool with the same hunter count lowers everyone's EV.

Why YTD loss totals signal burned bug classes

DeFiLlama's hacks feed shows 2238208290.928132 USD lost year to date across DeFi (DeFiLlama hacks API). Each incident teaches attackers and defenders the same lesson, so exploit patterns that appear there repeatedly are classes other hunters have already mined. Combined with altFINS' finding that roughly 72% of 2026 losses came from key and credential theft rather than contract bugs (altFINS), it points away from the most crowded bug classes.
