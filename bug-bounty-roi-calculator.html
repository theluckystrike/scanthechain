Worked example, symbolically

Take the calculator's three inputs: cost C, probability p, payout R. Expected value is p × R − C.

EV   = 0.05 × 50,000 − 4,000
     = 2,500 − 4,000
     = −1,500

ROI  = EV ÷ cost
     = −1,500 ÷ 4,000
     = −0.375  →  −37.5%

Negative. And that is the lesson the formula forces on you: below some probability threshold, even a generous payout table cannot rescue the campaign economics. Run it yourself in the tool above. The outputs recompute on every keystroke.

Two traps to keep your inputs honest. A rejected or duplicate submission contributes zero to expected value, so dead submissions must never be counted in p or R. And vulnerability details stay private through the responsible disclosure window, which delays when a payout actually lands.

FAQ

Why do most hunters earn nothing from a campaign?

Pool dynamics. A program's reward pool is finite. The payout distribution is a power law: one hunter takes the critical, everyone else takes a duplicate or nothing.

Immunefi's public bounty API exposes 237 programs, so competition concentrates on the same funded targets. Your expected value has to be computed net of that concentration, which is exactly what the p × payout − cost formula makes explicit.

Past campaign results do not predict future bounty payouts, and actual payouts may be zero.

Why does program count matter?

Because it bounds diversification. Running one campaign against one program is a lottery ticket; running parallel campaigns across more of the 237 programs exposed by Immunefi's public API spreads your fixed infrastructure cost across more draws, without changing the per-draw probability. The calculator's cost input should include that shared infra, priced honestly.

What does the year-to-date exploit total tell me?

The $1.83B lost year to date across DeFi, aggregated by DeFiLlama's hacks feed, is a map of what nobody caught yet. High totals in a category mean the bug class is still paying; a quiet quarter for, say, oracle manipulation after repeated hits usually means programs paid out, patched, and burned that class. Aim your p estimate where the losses still are.
